ISCA 2026 · DAC 2026 · ICCAD 2025 · 582 papers

What do this year’s chip-design conferences say about where the field is going?

Three conferences publish most new research on how chips and computers are designed, checked and built. This atlas sorts 582 of their papers by the problem each one tackles, explains each problem in plain words, and asks what the papers together say about the next few years. Here is the whole story in three sentences.

Who this is for, and what you need before you start

Who it is for

Engineers, researchers, students and technical leaders who want to know what chip and system design research is working on, without reading hundreds of papers.

What you need first

A rough idea of what a processor and memory are. Terms such as EDA (the software that turns a circuit description into a chip layout) are explained where they first appear.

What you will be able to do

Name the main research directions, find the papers behind each one, and say what evidence would confirm or disprove it.

Six directions to watch, each a hypothesis with a stated test

These come up again and again across themes and conferences. None is proven: each page names the paper that pushes back and the result that would disprove it.

  • Direction 1

    Requirements checked while designing

    A candidate is useful only when executable constraints can reject, repair, or rank it.

  • Direction 2

    Moving data is the real cost

    Useful computation is constrained by where data is, how far it moves, and what competes for the path.

  • Direction 3

    Measure, then decide the next design

    A design loop is only useful when sensing cost, model error, decision latency, and update semantics are explicit.

  • Direction 4

    Plan for faults and attacks early

    Performance without a declared fault and threat operating envelope is not a complete system result.

  • Direction 5

    Heat, wires and power in the loop

    An abstract optimum is irrelevant when the physical system cannot realize it.

  • Direction 6

    Better ways to measure results

    Every result is a measurement with a task population, evaluator, result, uncertainty, and failure boundary.

The common thread: design becomes a loop of measure, learn, decide and recheck

What each record was coded from

541 records rest on the short official program description.

Those give the problem and the claimed method, but rarely the test conditions. 16 papers were checked in depth against their full text or released files; see the deep reads.

So every direction here is a hypothesis to test, not a measured trend.
