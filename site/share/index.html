Walk someone through the course in five minutes

One step per part of the story. Or skip the talking and play the video.

  1. 0:00 · The question

    Open with the question.

    Open the big picture and read the question at the top.

    Say: “What do this year’s chip-design conferences say about where the field is going?”

  2. 0:30 · Three sentences

    Read the three sentences.

    Scroll to the three sentences and read each with the line under it.

    Say: “Design is becoming a loop; accelerators, memory and reliability lead; and most of this is still hypothesis.”

  3. 1:30 · One theme

    Show one theme with its picture.

    Open Themes, then memory, interconnect and storage.

    Say: “Moving data often costs more than computing it.”

  4. 2:30 · One direction

    Show one direction and its test.

    Open What to watch, then the closed-loop direction.

    Say: “Each direction names the result that would disprove it.”

  5. 3:30 · The caution

    Close on how deep the reading went.

    Back on the big picture, show the chart of what each record was coded from.

    Say: “Only 33 of 582 papers were read in full so far.”

Say where it comes from

Say it is an independent reading of public ISCA, DAC and ICCAD programs and papers, not an official conference page, and that most records come from program descriptions.

Built from 582 paper records coded from ISCA 2026, DAC 2026 and ICCAD 2025 (ICCAD 2026 papers were not yet public). Most records come from official program descriptions; see “How deep the reading went”. The papers belong to their authors.
