The pinned release self-identifies with the exact HLS (high-level synthesis: turning ordinary program code into hardware) paper title and DAC edition. Author linkage and headline reproduction remain unresolved. The assessment records source locators.

The inspected entry point samples configurations once, then synthesizes the batch. It does not implement measured-result feedback into further sampling in that function.

This does not rule out learned reasoning or other research flows. It does stop us calling this entry point a demonstrated search that learns from its results.

Its extracted metrics are synthesis-estimated latency (the wait for one result) and separate FPGA (a chip whose circuits can be rewired after it is made) resource fractions. They do not establish physical area, board speed, or a particular Pareto-quality improvement.

Defaults select AES and three samples, not evidence that the headline experiments used that budget. Cleanup removes trial reports and logs; a reproduction should retain these for audit.

No artifact code was run.

Research implication: distinguish reasoning encoded during training, one-shot proposal quality, and online feedback-driven search. Compare them using matched feasibility checks and full evaluation budgets before claiming a field direction.
