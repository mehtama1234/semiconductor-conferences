HLSArchOpt: what reading the full source showed

The author faculty page links this exact paper to HLSArchOpt at the inspected commit. The release supplies a Fire-module example, not a demonstrated full optimization workflow. README lists Vivado/HLS 2019.2, ZCU102 and a 100 MHz target.

Selected source inspection shows a dataflow pipeline with squeeze and parallel expand branches. The HLS script selects a 10 ns clock and enables synthesis/export, but comments out C simulation and co-simulation.

This is not evidence that the authors never tested correctness; it means this script does not reproduce those checks. No code was executed in this assessment.

The official description does not supply enough conditions to reconstruct the reported average gain. Do not extrapolate this example's platform to all benchmarks or treat its release as replication. Full-model coverage, LLM prompts/budgets, functional tests, resource limits and measurement modality remain unresolved.

The structured assessment pins the tree and inspected paths. Review remains pending; numerical synthesis is withheld until comparable benchmark conditions are available.

Stored synthesis reports

The pinned text and XML reports agree on selected fields: latency 335,494 cycles (reported as 3.355 ms at a 10 ns target), interval 229,703 cycles, and estimated clock period 8.419 ns. Resource estimates are 1,122 BRAM_18K, 1,840 DSP48E, 214,730 FF and 172,278 LUT.

File hashes and locators are in the assessment.

These are stored HLS estimates, not post-route timing or fresh measurements. Latency and launch interval must remain distinct; neither includes a demonstrated full-model or host-transfer boundary.

The report timestamp is May 2, 2025, before the pinned release; source-to-report reproducibility remains unverified. There is no matched baseline here from which to recover the paper's average speedup.
