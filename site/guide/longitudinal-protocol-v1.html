This protocol defines how the project will distinguish a recurring capability, mechanism persistence, directional prevalence, and comparative impact. It is the guardrail between the current evidence-backed snapshot and future long-term claims.

Unit of analysis

The unit is a paper–mechanism–condition record, not a title, keyword, session, or conference count. A record is comparable only when its mechanism and the conditions under which it was evaluated are coded.

The required comparison tuple is:

conference + edition + population + taxonomy path + mechanism + evidence tier
+ workload/design corpus + hardware/process + baseline + metric + budget
+ limits + independent review status

Claim ladder

The ladder of claims, from weakest to strongest

Claim level What it can say Gate
Capability A mechanism is present in a source-backed record One or more permitted source-backed records
Mechanism persistence A mechanism recurs across editions Same taxonomy, at least two editions, evidence tiers disclosed
Directional prevalence A mechanism becomes more/less common Complete denominators, equivalent populations, matched tiers, missingness analysis, independent review
Comparative impact One mechanism performs better Matched workload, hardware, baseline (the existing method a new result is compared against), metric, budget, extracted conditions, independent review

Title frequency can route investigation but cannot satisfy the last three levels.

Missingness and denominators

For every comparison, report five denominators separately:

  1. inventory records;
  2. records with permitted source text;
  3. technically coded records;
  4. condition-complete records;
  5. independently reviewed records.

Unavailable historical descriptions are not negative evidence. If a claim changes under plausible assignments of missing records, publish the sensitivity range or an insufficient-evidence result.

Direction-specific promotion tests

Direction Promotion test
Constraints become executable Held-out design improvement at equal correctness, synthesis, model-call, retry, and wall-clock budgets
Data movement as contract Benefit survives matched workload, quality target, capacity, bandwidth (how many bytes a link can move per second), topology, and migration cost
Reliability/security upstream Useful throughput (how much work is finished per second) or correctness remains improved after protection, recovery, and residual-risk costs
Physical reality in the loop Fast physical feedback improves feasible decisions against held-out higher-fidelity simulation or measurement
Closed-loop design Adaptive feedback beats a static baseline at equal sensing, calibration, and evaluation budget
Evaluation as research Independent reruns preserve the result or ranking under versioned evaluator semantics

The full machine-readable gates, required fields, and current boundary are in data/longitudinal-protocol-v1.json.

Current verdict

What the protocol concludes today

The current repository supports capability-level and mechanism-level synthesis. It cannot yet support claims about how common a direction is, or which matters more.

Sources differ in depth, many DAC 2025 records are missing, the samples differ, and there are no independent verdicts yet. This is a result of the protocol, not an invitation to fill gaps with assumptions.
