How to read the atlas

Expanded taxonomy atlas v1

The ten canonical themes remain the stable top-level spine. This atlas exposes the finer structure actually present in the coded corpus instead of treating the starter taxonomy as exhaustive.

The deduplicated selected sample contains 582 records, 113 observed subthemes, and 577 distinct mechanism labels. The original starter taxonomy named only 30 subthemes; 87 additional observed labels are now navigable here.

What these counts mean

These are counts of coded records and labels in the selected ISCA, DAC, and ICCAD sample. They are not publication prevalence, venue importance, temporal growth, or proof that two similarly named labels are distinct research fields.

A subtheme is an observed coding label; a mechanism is the paper-level technical differentiator. Read each record through its source tier, conditions, baselines, metrics, and limits.

How to use the expanded layer

  1. Start with a canonical theme to understand the first-principles boundary.
  2. Use this registry to find the observed subtheme and representative paper IDs.
  3. Open the theme page for source-backed paper cards and condition gaps.
  4. Move from the paper mechanism to the five high-value cross-cutting subtheme pages and six long-term direction pages only when the evidence supports that connection.

Taxonomy maintenance rule

Do not split a label merely because titles differ, and do not merge labels merely because they share a workload. Promote an observed subtheme into a stable conceptual category only after at least two records expose the same mechanism boundary, or after a primary-source review demonstrates that the apparent distinction is real.

Preserve aliases and uncertainty in the machine registry.
