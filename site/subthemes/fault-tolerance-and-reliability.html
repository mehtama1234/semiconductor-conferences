The bigger theme this belongs to

  • Power reliability and security

Spend representation bits on precision or protection

A floating-point representation divides its bits among fields controlling sign, numerical scale, and precision. An error in a scale field can have a very different numerical effect from an error in a low-significance precision bit.

A reliability-oriented representation can therefore allocate protection unevenly, but must state what accuracy is sacrificed and which faults remain possible.

SERA-Float makes this tradeoff within a 32-bit word. The recovered manuscript describes an eight-bit compressed mantissa and additional protected exponent/control information. The word remains 32 bits: this is not a claim of reducing stored width below FP32.

The primary-source recovery assessment preserves the inspected manuscript version, page numbering, conditions, and unresolved issues. Its extraction covers PDF pages 4–9 including the HAL cover in the page count; it is not an experimental reproduction.

The correction guarantee has a precise boundary

Section III.B and Figure 2, on PDF pages 4–5, describe two separate Hamming codewords protecting exponent information. The stated two-flip correction condition requires one flip in each protected half. It does not cover arbitrary two flips within one codeword.

The mantissa has parity for detection rather than correction. Detecting that a value is suspect does not by itself recover the original value; the larger computation needs an appropriate response.

Consequently, the format should not be described as correcting every two-bit fault or making approximate arithmetic immune to corruption.

The important conceptual distinction is between reducing fault impact through representation and proving a complete recovery mechanism. Both can be useful, but they support different claims about correct operation.

Injection experiments define the tested exposure

Table IV and Section IV.A describe modeled storage faults. The captured conditions use single-bit event percentages from 1% to 4%, with additional spatial double-bit events from 0.02% to 0.08%. These are injected experimental settings, not measured device failure rates.

An equal injection percentage across formats also needs a clear denominator and sampling procedure before it can be interpreted as equal physical exposure. Different stored widths and field layouts can change which bits are affected.

The record does not establish the injection implementation or its physical calibration.

The application set includes image processing and classification, with ResNet-50 and CIFAR-100 stated in the manuscript. Table VIII on PDF page 8 reports 98.85% FP32 baseline (the existing method a new result is compared against) accuracy, 97.90% SERA accuracy without faults, and 88.05% for SERA under the F2E4 setting.

These imply a 0.95-percentage-point no-fault loss and a further 9.85-point loss under that fault setting. They are author-reported values; dataset split and checkpoint provenance remain unverified.

Hardware savings are not complete-system savings

Section IV.C and Table IX, on PDF pages 8–9, describe synthesized multiplication hardware using Synopsys Design Compiler and a NanGate 45nm library at 1.10 V and 25°C. This is synthesis evidence, not measured silicon.

The displayed delays are 3.60 ns for the baseline and 1.18 ns for SERA, both longer than the stated 1 ns constraint. Setting that constraint does not demonstrate meeting it. It would be incorrect to infer 1 GHz operation from the constraint alone.

The recovery also records an accounting distinction: the headline energy saving is consistent with multiplying displayed power and delay, but differs from the ratio of the rounded energy-column values. Unrounded values and complete conversion/protection-module accounting are not established.

No full-inference or system-memory energy result follows from the per-multiplication comparison.

What this paper establishes as a research question

SERA-Float provides a concrete representation-level approach to combining approximation and selective protection. Its limits are equally concrete: unchanged stored width, conditional exponent correction, mantissa detection without correction, and application degradation under faults.

The next decisive evaluation would reproduce the injection procedure, resolve hardware accounting, and measure complete application quality and cost under a calibrated fault model. The current manuscript recovery supports a detailed mechanism explanation, not independent acceptance of its headline savings or a general reliability guarantee.

Compare the results carefully

How to compare these papers’ results fairly

First identify the inputs and required output quality. Then identify the limited resource: time, memory, communication, energy, circuit area, or correctness coverage.

Ask which part of the method reduces that cost and which new cost it adds. Compare numbers only when the experiments hold these conditions sufficiently similar.

A conference description may explain the proposal while omitting the full experiment. An author preprint link may have been coded from its abstract. The source classification does not by itself establish that every table was read. Missing locations remain visible below.

SERA-Float: A Soft Error Resilient Approximate Floating-Point Computing Format

ICCAD 2025 · Record: iccad-2025-1109

Reconciles the SERA-Float card with the recovered HAL manuscript and page-level assessment; separates selective exponent correction from mantissa detection, injected storage faults from physical failure rates, approximate-format quality from faulted quality, and synthesized multiplier cost from complete-system energy or timing closure.

This editorial correction updates the current reading view and connected dossiers, not the historical adjudication or scientific acceptance status. The original captured fields are preserved below.

Correction register

Problem:

Approximate floating-point representations can reduce numerical precision while storage bit flips can corrupt exponent, control or mantissa fields; the design question is which bits to protect and what accuracy and recovery guarantees remain.

Mechanism / artifact:

SERA-Float keeps a 32-bit word, compresses the mantissa to eight bits, and uses protected exponent/control information. Two separate (7,4) Hamming codewords protect exponent information; the stated two-flip correction condition is one flip in each protected half.

Mantissa parity detects an error but does not reconstruct the original mantissa.

Workload — the work tested:

Image sharpening, Gaussian smoothing, KNN, ResNet-50 and linear SVM; CIFAR-100 is stated for classification. Storage faults are injected as F1 single-bit events at 1%, 2%, 3% and 4%, with F2 adding spatial double-bit events at 0.02%, 0.04%, 0.06% and 0.08%.

Hardware and manufacturing assumptions:

Verilog multiplier synthesis with Synopsys Design Compiler, NanGate 45nm, 1.10 V, 25 C and a stated 1 ns constraint. The captured Table IX delays are 3.60 ns for the baseline and 1.18 ns for SERA, so the constraint is not demonstrated timing closure.

No measured silicon or full-memory/inference (running a trained AI model to get answers) implementation is established.

Reported measurements:

Table VIII reports FP32 baseline accuracy 98.85%, SERA accuracy without faults 97.90%, and SERA under F2E4 88.05%; this is a 0.95-point no-fault loss and a further 9.85-point faulted loss in the displayed setting.

Table IX reports synthesized per-multiplication delay of 3.60 ns for baseline and 1.18 ns for SERA under the stated library and voltage/temperature conditions.

The paper reports multiplier-level power/energy comparisons; the recovered assessment finds that the headline energy calculation and rounded energy-column ratio are not identical, so unrounded values and complete protection/conversion accounting are required before accepting a system-energy claim.

Limits on the conclusion:

The exponent guarantee is conditional: arbitrary two flips in one Hamming codeword are outside the stated correction condition. Mantissa parity detects rather than corrects, so detection is not recovery.

The percentages are injected experiment settings, not measured device failure rates. The denominator, spatial/temporal distribution, injection implementation and physical calibration are not established in the recovered evidence.

The 32-bit word is not storage-width compression below FP32. SERA has a no-fault accuracy cost and a larger reported degradation under the fault setting; protection does not make approximate computation immune to corruption.

A 1 ns synthesis constraint does not prove 1 GHz operation when displayed delays exceed 1 ns. Per-multiplier synthesized cost is not complete inference, memory, conversion, protection or system energy. Dataset split, checkpoint provenance, raw unrounded tables and independent execution remain open.
